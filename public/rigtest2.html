MOUSE STAND-IN — this is not head tracking

rigtest2 setup

Five steps, a few minutes. Everything is saved in this browser as you go. Press S at any time to come back here, Esc to go to the rig view. Steps 1 to 4 get the rig running; step 5 replaces the guessed camera angles with measured ones, and is the one that stops the hologram swimming as you move.

Pick the two webcams and aim them

Both webcams watch your head; the pair is the only head tracker. Press Start, then mark one camera left and the other right — left is the one on your left as you face the rig. Aim each one until the blue dots sit on your eyes in both pictures at once. Two webcams of the same model report the same name, so tell them apart by covering one lens and watching which picture goes dark, not by the label. Getting them the wrong way round halves the parallax; the page now measures how far the two cameras' answers miss each other and refuses the eye rather than drawing a plausible wrong one, but it can only see that once you move off the centre line. (A ZED on the base is for hands later; leave it off here.)

Where the pair is

Measured from the centre of the rig, which is the middle of the acrylic sheet. The two cameras are symmetric about it. Height is measured from the sheet, so 0 means "level with the sheet". The baseline, height, depth and where your head sits are the measurements; the two angles are worked out from them and follow them as you edit, because in step 1 you aimed the cameras at your own face and these numbers have to describe that same aim. Type an angle only if you really measured it with a protractor — after that they stop following, and the page will say so if they drift apart.

The rig

These already describe the rig as measured: a 16:9 panel above at 45 degrees facing down and toward you, the sheet 6 inches below the panel, black paper 6 inches below the sheet, the whole screen used, the picture sent flipped. Change them only if the rig changes.

Trim the tracker's origin

Go to the rig view, put your head where you normally sit, and look at the cube standing on the base. Move your head left and right. If the cube swims — slides about on the base instead of staying put — nudge the trim until it stops. This trims where the TRACKER thinks its origin is, not the rig. If the hologram is in the wrong place from every angle equally, that is a rig number in step 3, and trimming it away here will only make it swim from somewhere else.

Measure the pair from your own face

The two angles in step 2 are worked out from where you said your head sits — they are a good guess, not a measurement, and a guess that is one degree off moves your tracked head about a centimetre at arm's length. That is not something the trim in step 4 can fix: a trim is a constant shift, and an angle error shifts you differently depending on where you stand, which is exactly what "the hologram swims" means. So measure the angles instead. Your face is the calibration target. Both cameras already find the same 478 points on it, so moving your head round the volume gives hundreds of matched points per second and no printed pattern to hold. Press Start capture, then move about where you normally sit: left and right, nearer and further, higher and lower. The flag on the glass says what is still missing. Then press Solve. The scale cannot come from your face — two cameras watching a face can only see their pose relative to each other, never how big the world is — so it comes from the baseline you measured in step 2. Measure that one with a tape.

Then prove it, without taking anything on faith

Put your head at the spot you told the page about in step 2 (), hold still, and this reports how far the measured pair thinks you are from it. A calibration that cannot survive that is not a calibration. Whatever constant offset is left is what step 4's trim is for — the trim still exists, and it still does the same job.
